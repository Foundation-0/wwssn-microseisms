## The project

- **Question.** Did ocean wave energy, as recorded in seismic microseisms, change between 1962–78 and today?
- **In plain words.** Ocean waves shake the sea floor and make the whole Earth hum faintly. Seismometers record this
  as microseisms. Modern records show wave energy rising. The World-Wide Standardized Seismographic Network (WWSSN)
  recorded on photographic paper and film from the 1960s, and the US Geological Survey (USGS) has released scans of
  part of that archive. Measuring microseisms on those scans would extend the record back by decades.
- **Hub.** [physics0](https://github.com/Foundation-0/physics0), which covers physics and Earth science and holds
  inventories of the seismic and climate archives.
- **Related pattern.** [plate-eclipses](https://github.com/Foundation-0/plate-eclipses): an old photographic archive
  joined to modern data.
- **Status.** Not started.
- **Lead.** To be named.

## Checked on 19 Oct 2026

- The USGS released "World-Wide Standardized Seismographic Network film chip scans" on 14 July 2025
  ([data release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/world-wide-standardized-seismographic-network-film-chip-scans)).
  - Licence: CC0 1.0 Universal.
  - DOI: 10.5066/P13PHZMA.
  - About 3.7 million single-day records from 1962–78, about 5% of the archive.

## From the scouting report, not yet re-checked

### Earlier work

- Aster et al. found wave energy rising by 0.27% a year at 52 stations since the late 1980s. They wrote that
  extending the record into the analog era, before digital recording, "offers an opportunity"
  ([Nature Communications 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10620394/)).
- The USGS says "few researchers have utilized large numbers of these records"
  ([Nagle-McNaughton 2022](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70231381)).
- Studies of single stations' analog records exist:
  - Bromirski 2023: Berkeley, 1931–2021;
  - Lecocq et al. 2020: Belgium, the 1953 storm.
- No study of microseisms at many stations using the WWSSN scans since their release was found.
- The USGS authors (Aster, Ringler, Lee) are the likeliest competitors and collaborators.

### Tools and data

- DigitSeis, software that turns scanned seismograms into digital traces.
- Lee (2022): digitised waveforms.
- Peterson and Hutt 2014: instrument responses, which give how each instrument turned ground motion into a trace.
- ERA5 wave reanalysis, a reconstruction of past ocean waves, to compare against.
- EarthScope's FDSN web services, for modern seismic data.
- Access to the scans is through Globus, a service for moving large files.

### Risks

- The 5% may have been chosen around earthquakes.
- The metadata gives 1961–1996, while the summary says 1962–78.
- Instrument gains changed over time.

### When to stop

The project stops if no station has about 3 years of near-continuous days, or if the amplitudes don't track ERA5.
